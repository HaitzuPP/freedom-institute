Funding and credibility

Our largest funder does not get a soft review; we wrote that into the structure before we wrote a word of research.

UR would be the Institute's primary supporter and its founding organiser, with funding and in-kind contributions disclosed in full. That relationship creates the obvious conflict, because UR builds the category of product the Institute exists to assess. Independence that depends on goodwill is not independence, so control of methodology and publication sits with a majority-independent board and autonomous research leadership, and any assessment touching UR or anything the Institute builds is led by external reviewers.

Disclosure

Every funder, grant, and in-kind contribution published with amounts and dates.

Control

Majority-independent board; research leadership holds final say on what publishes.

Conflicts

External reviewers lead any assessment touching a funder or an Institute product.

No paid rankings

No sponsored placement, no affiliate revenue, no sponsor veto over a finding.

The organiser

UR builds the products; the Institute is the check on them

UR is a global institute for privacy and encryption products. Its network is contributed rather than rented: more than 80,000 members supply bandwidth that anonymises traffic for people who cannot route around their own government. UR would organise and fund the Freedom Institute, and would also sit inside its scope of assessment.

A contributed network changes who holds the risk

Commercial privacy infrastructure concentrates trust in one operator and one jurisdiction; a member-contributed network spreads it across the people using it. That design is the reason UR can serve places where a single corporate exit node would be blocked or seized within a week, and it is also the reason UR's own claims need testing by someone who does not report to UR.

The Institute publishes the test results either way. A finding against UR is the proof that the structure works.
