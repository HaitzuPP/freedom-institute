Privacy is a promise until someone tests it.

The Freedom Institute grades how countries censor, measures what privacy products actually do with your data, and publishes the evidence behind every score. We build the testing tools in the open so anyone can repeat the work.

What we are for

If you can be identified, you can be targeted; most people have no way to find out which tools actually stop that.

A VPN provider represents that it retains no logs, a messaging platform represents that its users cannot be identified, and a cloud service represents that deleted data has been deleted. Each of these is a representation made by the party holding the commercial interest in it being believed, and almost none of them arrive with evidence that a reader can verify independently. The same gap runs through state conduct: a government restricts a protocol on a Tuesday and restores it on a Friday, and the record of what happened has usually disappeared before anyone documents it.

We publish country censorship grades and a Privacy Product Index that separate what a provider says from what we observed, what an auditor confirmed, and what nobody knows yet. The unknowns get printed too.

  • 01Advance privacy, freedom of expression, and access to information through evidence, technology, and advocacy.
  • 02Serve the people choosing privacy tools, the journalists who depend on them, and the civil-society groups working under pressure.
  • 03Hand policymakers and product teams recommendations specific enough to act on.
Who this is for

Three readers decide whether this work was worth doing

Every grade and every index entry is written so that these three can make a decision with it.

Journalists and researchers

They need to know what a device gives up when it is inspected and what a provider hands over when it is asked, before the trip rather than after it.

Organisers and civil society

They coordinate in real time under conditions where group membership is the sensitive fact and the connection itself may be the evidence against them.

Households making an ordinary choice

They are picking between two services on a Sunday afternoon and deserve a straight answer on which one collects less, keeps it for a shorter time, and lets them take it back.

    People choose the tools that guard their lives on the strength of a slogan

    No-logs is a marketing line until someone runs the test

    Providers write their own privacy claims and grade their own homework. Independent verification is rare, expensive, and almost never repeated after launch, so a claim that was true at audit can quietly stop being true and nobody finds out.

    Shutdowns end before the record of them begins

    A throttle lasts six hours; a block on one protocol lasts a weekend. By the time a report is written the traffic has recovered and the evidence is gone. Without continuous measurement, the worst behaviour is the behaviour that leaves no trace.

    The people most at risk get the least usable guidance

    A journalist in Tehran, an organiser in Yangon, and a researcher in Minsk all need the same thing: which of these five tools will not give me up, and how do you know. The honest answer today is usually a forum thread and a review paid for by affiliate revenue.

    Core activities

    Three products, one rule: show the evidence or mark it unknown

    01 / Measure freedom

    Grades carry their dates, their coverage, and their confidence

    Country scores cover blocking, shutdowns, restrictions on circumvention, and punishment for online expression. Where our coverage is thin, the confidence bar says so rather than rounding the gap into a number.

    • Continuous network measurement
    • Event log with sources and timestamps
    • Quarterly re-grade, public changelog
    02 / Evaluate products

    Provider claims and observed behaviour are scored separately

    The Privacy Product Index compares tools inside a category on data collection, retention, provider access, tracking, privacy defaults, and user control; each cell is labelled claimed, observed, audited, or unknown.

    • Category-relative scoring
    • Traffic and binary analysis
    • No paid placement, no affiliate links
    03 / Build and advocate

    Open tools let anyone repeat the measurement

    We ship the diagnostics we test with, work with product teams on the fixes our testing surfaces, and turn findings into guidance people can act on and briefings regulators can use.

    • Open-source testing harness
    • Coordinated disclosure to vendors
    • Plain-language buyer guidance

    Funding and credibility

    Our largest funder does not get a soft review; we wrote that into the structure before we wrote a word of research.

    UR would be the Institute's primary supporter and its founding organiser, with funding and in-kind contributions disclosed in full. That relationship creates the obvious conflict, because UR builds the category of product the Institute exists to assess. Independence that depends on goodwill is not independence, so control of methodology and publication sits with a majority-independent board and autonomous research leadership, and any assessment touching UR or anything the Institute builds is led by external reviewers.

    Disclosure

    Every funder, grant, and in-kind contribution published with amounts and dates.

    Control

    Majority-independent board; research leadership holds final say on what publishes.

    Conflicts

    External reviewers lead any assessment touching a funder or an Institute product.

    No paid rankings

    No sponsored placement, no affiliate revenue, no sponsor veto over a finding.

    The organiser

    UR builds the products; the Institute is the check on them

    UR is a global institute for privacy and encryption products. Its network is contributed rather than rented: more than 80,000 members supply bandwidth that anonymises traffic for people who cannot route around their own government. UR would organise and fund the Freedom Institute, and would also sit inside its scope of assessment.

    Organiser and primary supporter
    ur.xyz

    A contributed network changes who holds the risk

    Commercial privacy infrastructure concentrates trust in one operator and one jurisdiction; a member-contributed network spreads it across the people using it. That design is the reason UR can serve places where a single corporate exit node would be blocked or seized within a week, and it is also the reason UR's own claims need testing by someone who does not report to UR.

    The Institute publishes the test results either way. A finding against UR is the proof that the structure works.
