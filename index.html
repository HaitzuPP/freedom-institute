Public-interest privacy research

Privacy is a promise until someone tests it.

The Freedom Institute grades how countries censor, measures what privacy products actually do with your data, and publishes the evidence behind every score. We build the testing tools in the open so anyone can repeat the work.

First edition The Privacy Product Index opens for public methodology review Sep 2026
What we are for

If you can be identified, you can be targeted; most people have no way to find out which tools actually stop that.

A VPN says no logs. A messenger says anonymous. A cloud service says deleted. Every one of those is a claim, and almost none of them arrive with evidence a reader can check. Meanwhile a government blocks a protocol on a Tuesday, restores it on a Friday, and the record of what happened is gone by the time anyone writes it up.

We publish country censorship grades and a Privacy Product Index that separate what a provider says from what we observed, what an auditor confirmed, and what nobody knows yet. The unknowns get printed too.

  • 01Advance privacy, freedom of expression, and access to information through evidence, technology, and advocacy.
  • 02Serve the people choosing privacy tools, the journalists who depend on them, and the civil-society groups working under pressure.
  • 03Hand policymakers and product teams recommendations specific enough to act on.

People choose the tools that guard their lives on the strength of a slogan

No-logs is a marketing line until someone runs the test

Providers write their own privacy claims and grade their own homework. Independent verification is rare, expensive, and almost never repeated after launch, so a claim that was true at audit can quietly stop being true and nobody finds out.

Shutdowns end before the record of them begins

A throttle lasts six hours; a block on one protocol lasts a weekend. By the time a report is written the traffic has recovered and the evidence is gone. Without continuous measurement, the worst behaviour is the behaviour that leaves no trace.

The people most at risk get the least usable guidance

A journalist in Tehran, an organiser in Yangon, and a researcher in Minsk all need the same thing: which of these five tools will not give me up, and how do you know. The honest answer today is usually a forum thread and a review paid for by affiliate revenue.

Core activities

Three products, one rule: show the evidence or mark it unknown

01 / Measure freedom

Grades carry their dates, their coverage, and their confidence

Country scores cover blocking, shutdowns, restrictions on circumvention, and punishment for online expression. Where our coverage is thin, the confidence bar says so rather than rounding the gap into a number.

  • Continuous network measurement
  • Event log with sources and timestamps
  • Quarterly re-grade, public changelog
02 / Evaluate products

Provider claims and observed behaviour are scored separately

The Privacy Product Index compares tools inside a category on data collection, retention, provider access, tracking, privacy defaults, and user control; each cell is labelled claimed, observed, audited, or unknown.

  • Category-relative scoring
  • Traffic and binary analysis
  • No paid placement, no affiliate links
03 / Build and advocate

Open tools let anyone repeat the measurement

We ship the diagnostics we test with, work with product teams on the fixes our testing surfaces, and turn findings into guidance people can act on and briefings regulators can use.

  • Open-source testing harness
  • Coordinated disclosure to vendors
  • Plain-language buyer guidance

Research programme

Privacy Claims vs Reality is where we start

The signature series takes the three promises the industry repeats most often, no logs, anonymous, and deleted, and tests each one against what the software and the servers actually do. Everything after it follows the same rule: publish the method, publish the failures, publish the unknowns.
